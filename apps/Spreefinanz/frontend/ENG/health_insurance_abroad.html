What should you pay attention to?

“Health insurance abroad” sounds simple – in practice, details decide whether you are really covered. Pay particular attention to the scope, scope of services and the rules for return, termination and tariff changes.

01 Scope, travel duration and countries (USA/Canada!)

Check in which countries the protection applies and whether there are restrictions in regions with high costs (e.g. USA/Canada). Equally important: Does the tariff match your planned length of stay (weeks, months, several years) - and what happens if you extend your stay or return to Germany in between?

02 Scope of services: outpatient, inpatient, return transport & deductible

Good tariffs differ not only in price, but in the services: doctor's visits, hospital, medication, diagnostics, dental, pregnancy, psychotherapy or rehabilitation. Also pay attention to sensible rules regarding medically appropriate repatriation, direct reimbursement of costs (where possible) and how deductibles, limits and exclusions are formulated.

03 Pre-existing conditions, sport/risks and digital processing

Pre-existing illnesses, planned treatments or high-risk sports must be carefully taken into account - otherwise there is a risk of performance gaps. Also check how you submit invoices (app/portal), how quickly reimbursements are made and whether you can get easy support along the way if something really happens.

What mistakes can you make when choosing?

The most common problems do not arise “in an emergency”, but rather when choosing a tariff. These mistakes later cost time, money and nerves - and can easily be avoided with the right preparation.

Just decide based on price

Cheap tariffs seem attractive, but often have severe restrictions: low reimbursement rates, exclusions for pre-existing conditions, limitations on diagnostics or a lack of options for longer stays. What matters is whether the benefits match your country and your risk - not just the monthly contribution.

USA/Canada, sports or family not planned properly

Many tariffs vary greatly depending on expensive regions and risks such as diving, motorcycling, trekking or working abroad. Family constellations, pregnancy or planned treatments must also be taken into account in advance so that there are no unpleasant surprises later.

Ignore return to Germany

A typical stumbling block: You are perfectly protected abroad, but when you return there are no transitional rules or follow-up solutions. It is important to have clear answers to questions such as: When does the protection begin, how do you end it, what happens if you return temporarily - and what options are there afterwards?

What is the difference between foreign health insurance and international health insurance?

Classic international health insurance is often intended for trips and limited periods of time (e.g. vacation or a few months). International health insurance is usually designed for longer stays, emigration or permanent living abroad and can be significantly more comprehensive depending on the tariff.

Does health insurance abroad also apply in the USA and Canada?

That depends on the tariff. Many providers exclude the USA/Canada or charge significantly higher contributions. If these countries are relevant to you (even just for stopovers), this should be planned for from the start.

What about pre-existing conditions – can I still get protection?

That is individual. Some tariffs exclude certain diagnoses, others check and include surcharges or waiting times. It is important to be honest and complete so that the insurance coverage is effective in the event of a claim.

Do I also need travel insurance or is health insurance enough abroad?

Health insurance abroad covers medical costs. Travel insurance (e.g. trip cancellation, luggage, assistance) are separate components. Whether this makes sense depends on how you travel (long-term/backpacking, family, business trips) and what risk you want to insure.
